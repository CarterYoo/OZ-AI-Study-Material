Three distinctions to keep clear

Input x is a car's measured weight. It does not change when you choose a model.

w and b are the slope and intercept of the line you choose. These are the parameters moving on the right plot.

minimizew J(w) chooses w while holding b fixed. Here J(w) abbreviates J(w, b fixed). The expression fw(x) = wx without b is specifically the case b = 0.

minimizew,b J(w,b) chooses both w and b. More lines are available, so the achievable minimum cost on the same data cannot exceed the minimum with only w free. The two minima could be equal.

1/2 halves all scores without moving the minimizer. 1/m takes their average. Squaring prevents positive and negative errors from cancelling and gives larger errors more weight.

Dataset and selected cars

R. Quinlan (1993), UCI Auto MPG, DOI 10.24432/C5859H, CC BY 4.0. Eight evenly spaced observations by weight are selected from 398 public observations, using the seaborn-data copy. Fuel economy is measured in mpg; higher is better. Weight alone cannot explain every difference. This lab teaches the cost function rather than evaluating predictive performance.
